# Visual coaching review

Follow ready state, direction recognition, shoulder/racket preparation while moving, final adjustments, contact, follow-through and preparation for the next ball. Compare a comfortable and a challenged shot when explaining a recurring difficulty.

Separate three layers:

1. **Observation:** visible continuous change with time/evidence ID, such as a last large step overlapping the swing.
2. **Interpretation:** a plausible explanation with confidence and alternatives. Moving contact may be necessary on a wide ball.
3. **Practice/check:** a controlled change and an observable outcome that tests the player's reported difficulty.

Preserve useful movements: complete follow-through, visible split preparation, or good consecutive-shot transition. Do not replace individual evidence with a generic beginner checklist.

## Common traps

- Racket movement alone is not coordinated unit turn; examine shoulders, non-dominant hand and feet without inventing joint angles.
- Bent elbows and open stances are not inherently wrong; check comfortable spacing and repeatability.
- A finished pose does not prove pre-contact acceleration. Inspect adjacent frames.
- A lifted foot does not alone prove poor balance; distinguish controlled rising from an uncontrolled chase.
- Upright waiting in feeding intervals does not establish slow rally recovery. Opponent contact may be offscreen.
- Oblique projection does not establish three-dimensional crowding. Consider motion/context and a side view.
- Successful-contact montages cannot establish miss rates or every cause of long/net balls. Retain failures and landing evidence.
- Other-court balls/sounds can be confused with the subject. Do not locate impact from sound alone.

## Practice priorities

For newer players, select a few supported changes rather than simultaneously changing grip, stance, swing size, wrist action and power. When justified, start with shoulder/left-hand coordination and comfortable spacing, then connect to the next ball. Reduce feeding speed/variation to test one change before increasing demands.

Specify manageable repetitions, one cue, target and outcome. Proposed practice schedules are suggestions, not research findings or guaranteed improvement. Recovery depends on likely reply geometry; a fixed feeding marker is a drill simplification, not a universal return-to-center rule.
